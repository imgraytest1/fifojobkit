          Residents also pay the 2% Medicare levy, phased in (nil up to $28,011 for singles). Foreign residents and most working holiday makers don't pay it.
          
      
          
      
          Key points
          
      
            
        
          • Tax-free threshold: $18,200 for residents. With the Low Income Tax Offset (up to $700), residents pay no income tax until about $22,866.
            • 
        
          • Claim the tax-free threshold from one employer only.
            • 
        
          • Working holiday makers: no tax-free threshold and no tax offsets. The employer must be registered with the ATO to withhold at 15%; unregistered employers withhold at foreign resident rates.
            • 
        
          • Visa isn't the whole test: tax residency depends on the ATO residency tests (how long you're here, where you live), not the visa alone. 482 and student visa holders here 6+ months are usually residents.
            • 
        
          • No TFN given: 47% withheld for residents, 45% for foreign residents.
            • 
        
          • Super Guarantee: 12% of ordinary-time earnings, on top unless the pay is quoted "including super".
            • 
        
          • Recent changes: 19 / 32.5 / 37 / 45% to 2023–24; 16 / 30 / 37 / 45% from 1 Jul 2024 (Stage 3); 15 / 30 / 37 / 45% from 1 Jul 2026; bottom rate drops to 14% from 1 Jul 2027.
            • 
      
          
      
            
        
          • Estimates of annual tax, not exact payslip withholding. Assumes every rostered shift is worked for the full year.
            • 
        
          • Not included: HELP/HECS repayments, Medicare levy surcharge, deductions, salary sacrifice, zone tax offset, and unpaid travel days.
            • 
        
          • General information only, not tax advice. Sources: ATO resident rates, ATO foreign resident rates, ATO Schedule 15.

            Tips for a 1-year stay
            
        
              
          
            • Low fees matter most. In one year, fees and insurance can eat more than investment returns add.
              • 
          
            • Check the insurance. Most funds add life and injury insurance and take the cost from your super. Decide if you need it. Turning it off saves money.
              • 
          
            • One fund only. Give the same fund to every employer (they give you a "choice of fund" form). Two funds means two sets of fees.
              • 
          
            • Claim it when you leave. After your visa ends and you leave, claim online (DASP). You get 35% back. If you don't claim, the fund can pass it to the ATO, and you can still claim it there.

            Rules to know
            
      
              
        
            • 6 months per employer. You can usually work for one employer for up to 6 months (visa condition 8547). Check exceptions with Home Affairs.
              • 
        
            • Stay a 2nd or 3rd year. 88 days of "specified work" for a 2nd year, 179 days for a 3rd. Mining and construction can count if the postcode is on the regional list.
              • 
        
            • Tax return. Lodge one for each tax year you worked (July to June). Tax on income above $45,000 in a tax year is higher, so a year that crosses 30 June can mean less tax.
              • 
        
            • Super back (DASP). After you leave Australia and your visa ends, claim your super online. Working holiday super is taxed 65%, so you get 35% back. Low-fee funds for a 1-year stay →

                    Your rights at work
                    Every worker in Australia has the same basic rights, whatever your visa. Here is what to know, and who to call for free help.
                    
    
                    
      
                    💵 Pay
                    
      
                      
        
                    • The lowest legal pay for adults is $26.44 an hour ($1,004.90 for a 38-hour week), from 1 July 2026. Most FIFO trades get much more under an award or agreement.
                      • 
        
                    • You must get a payslip within 1 working day of payday. Check it in Pay & payslips.
                      • 
        
                    • Long unpaid "trial shifts" are usually against the law. A short skills test is fine.
                      • 
      
                    
      Fair Work pay calculator
    
                    
    
                    
      
                    🏦 Super
                    
      
                      
        
                    • Your employer pays 12% on top of your pay into your super fund.
                      • 
        
                    • New from 1 July 2026 (Payday Super): super must reach your fund within 7 business days of each payday. For a new job, the first payment can take up to 20 business days.
                      • 
        
                    • Check it in your super fund's app, or in myGov (ATO online services).
                      • 
      
                    
      About Payday Super
    
                    
    
                    
      
                    🦺 Safety
                    
      
                      
        
                    • You can stop or refuse work if you reasonably believe it puts you at serious, immediate risk. Tell your supervisor straight away. You may be given other safe work.
                      • 
        
                    • Your employer can't punish you for raising a safety problem.
                      • 
        
                    • Bullying, sexual harassment and too much fatigue are safety problems too. You can report them.
                      • 
        
                    • Report unsafe work to WorkSafe WA: 1300 307 877.
                      • 
      
                    
    
                    
    
                    
      
                    🌏 Visa holders
                    
      
                      
        
                    • You have the same work rights as Australians. A contract can't take them away.
                      • 
        
                    • Only the Department of Home Affairs can cancel a visa. Your boss can't.
                      • 
        
                    • If you are being underpaid or exploited, ask the Fair Work Ombudsman for help. Two Home Affairs pilot programs can protect your visa, or let you stay longer to finish your claim.
                      • 
      
                    
      
                    Visa protectionsHome Affairs: work rights
                    
    
                    
    
                    
      
                    🤝 Unions and fair treatment
                    
      
                      
        
                    • You can choose to join a union, or not. Your employer can't treat you badly either way.
                      • 
        
                    • Sacked and think it was unfair? You have only 21 days to apply to the Fair Work Commission.
                      • 
        
                    • Sexual harassment: employers must take steps to stop it. You can complain to your employer, the safety regulator or the Australian Human Rights Commission.
                      • 
      
                    
      Unfair dismissal: 21 days

                    Wellbeing on swing
                    FIFO life is hard on sleep, mood and family. These are free, private help lines and simple habits that help.
                    
    
                    
      
                    🆘 Need help now?
                    
      
                    
        Emergency: 000Danger to life. Police, fire, ambulance
        Lifeline · 13 11 1424/7 crisis support. Text 0477 13 11 14
        MATES · 1300 642 11124/7, for construction, mining and energy workers and families
      
                    
    
                    
    
                    
      
                    📞 More free help lines
                    
      
                    
        Beyond Blue · 1300 22 4636Stress, worry, low mood. 24/7
        Suicide Call Back Service · 1300 659 46724/7, also for people worried about someone
        1800RESPECT · 1800 737 732Sexual assault, family or domestic violence. 24/7
        13YARN · 13 92 76Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander crisis support
        QLife · 1800 184 527LGBTIQ+ peer support
      
                    
      
                    Most big employers also have an EAP (Employee Assistance Program): free, private counselling, often for your family too. Ask HR or check the camp noticeboard.
                    
    
                    
    
                    
      
                    😴 On swing
                    
      
                      
        
                    • Sleep: keep the same sleep times, use an eye mask and earplugs, and put your phone away at bedtime. Night shift? Sleep as soon as you get back to camp.
                      • 
        
                    • Neck pillow: bring your own contoured neck pillow from Kmart or Spotlight. Camp pillows are often flat or lumpy. A half-size one is best if you can find it: it packs small for the flight and fits the bunk.
                      • 
        
                    • Magnesium: some workers take a magnesium supplement in the evening to help with sleep and tight, cramping muscles. Food works too: nuts, seeds, leafy greens and wholegrains. Keep supplements under 350 mg a day (the US health limit for supplements).
                      • 
        
                    • Hard water: a lot of WA water is hard, which is the white calcite scale you see on a kettle element. It is safe to drink. Some people take magnesium to keep their calcium and magnesium in balance. Early animal research on kidney stones is promising, but it is not proven in people yet.
                      • 
        
                    • 🧳 Open the swing packing list
                      • 
        
                    • Fatigue: too tired to work safely? Tell your supervisor. Fatigue is a safety risk.
                      • 
        
                    • Heat: drink water all shift, not only when you are thirsty.
                      • 
        
                    • Wet mess: set your drink limit before you walk in. Big nights make early starts dangerous.
                      • 
        
                    • Move: 20 minutes at the camp gym or a walk helps sleep and mood.
                      • 
      
                    
    
                    
    
                    
      
                    🏠 Home and family
                    
      
                      
        
                    • Share your roster and flight dates. Use My dates to send them to your calendar.
                      • 
        
                    • Set a regular call time with home, like after dinner each day.
                      • 
        
                    • Plan your days off together, so the first day home isn't all jobs.
                      • 
        
                    • Make a money plan together. Swing goals helps.
                      • 
      
                    
      FIFO Families
    
                    
    
                    
      
                    🤝 Look out for your mates
                    
      
                    Signs: quieter than usual, drinking more, not sleeping, talking about being a burden. Use R U OK?'s 4 steps:
                    
      
                    1. Ask "Are you OK?"
                    2. Listen without judging.
                    3. Encourage action: a help line, the EAP or a doctor.
                    4. Check in again in a few days.

                          🩺 Medical test prep
                          Most FIFO and mining jobs need a pre-employment medical before you start. Here is what usually happens. Tests differ by job and employer, so check your offer letter or ask the clinic.
                          
    
                          
      
                          🧪 What is a lab test?
                          
      
                          A lab test is when a sample of your blood or urine is sent to a laboratory to be checked. A nurse or collector takes the sample at the clinic or a collection centre. The lab sends the result to the doctor, who decides if you are fit for the job.
                          
      
                          5–15 minto give the sample. Results usually take 1 to 3 working days. Some urine tests give a quick result on the spot.
                          
    
                          
    
                          
      
                          ⏱ How long does it all take?
                          
      
                            
        
                          • Basic medical: about 1 to 2 hours (questions, checks, hearing, urine, fitness).
                            • 
        
                          • With bloods or scans: can take half a day, depending on the clinic.
                            • 
        
                          • Who pays: the employer or agency usually books and pays. Ask before you book. If you pay yourself, keep the receipt.
                            • 
        
                          • Book early. Clinics fill up, and you often cannot start until the doctor signs you off.
                            • 
      
                          
    
                          
    
                          
      
                          🩸 Bloods and scans
                          
      
                          Blood test: a nurse puts a small needle in your arm and fills 1 to 3 small tubes. It takes a few minutes. It can check things like blood sugar, cholesterol, liver and kidneys. Only fast (no food) if the clinic tells you to. Drink water, and eat well the night before.
                          
      
                          Scans: not every job needs them. A chest X-ray may be asked for in dusty work. A back or joint scan is rare and only if the doctor or employer asks. You stand still for a few minutes and it does not hurt. Tell them if you might be pregnant.
                          
    
                          
    
                          
      
                          🏋️ The workout (fitness) test
                          
      
                          20–60 minThe clinic checks you can do the physical parts of the job safely.
                          
      
                            
        
                          • Lifting and carrying a set weight
                            • 
        
                          • Pushing, pulling, squatting, climbing steps
                            • 
        
                          • Balance, bending and reach
                            • 
        
                          • Sometimes a heart rate or step test
                            • 
      
                          
      
                          Tips: wear comfy clothes and closed shoes. Eat light 1 to 2 hours before. Tell them about any old injury or sore back, knees or shoulders. Stop and say so if something hurts.
                          
    
                          
    
                          
      
                          👂 The hearing test
                          
      
                          10–15 minYou sit in a quiet booth with headphones and press a button each time you hear a beep. It sets your hearing baseline for noisy sites.
                          
      
                          Tips: keep away from loud noise (music, power tools, motorbikes) for about 12 to 16 hours before. Tell them if you have a cold, blocked ears, an ear infection or wear a hearing aid.
                          
    
                          
    
                          
      
                          🧫 The urine test
                          
      
                          10–15 minThere are usually two parts: a drug and alcohol screen and a health check of the urine (sugar, protein and blood, which can show kidney or diabetes problems). Some sites also do a breath alcohol test.
                          
      
                            
        
                          1. Show photo ID.
                            2. 
        
                          2. Wash your hands. You may be asked to leave bags and jackets outside the toilet.
                            3. 
        
                          3. Give the sample in a private cubicle. The collector checks and seals it in front of you.
                            4. 
        
                          4. You sign the form. The sample goes to the lab.
                            5. 
      
                          
      
                          Tips: drink normal amounts of water. Do not drink a huge amount, because a watery sample can be rejected and you have to repeat it. Tell the collector and doctor about every medicine and supplement you take, including pills from the chemist. Bring the box or the script. Some medicines and foods (like poppy seeds) can cause a false positive, so honesty protects you.
                          
      
                          Some drugs stay in your body for days or weeks, so a test can be positive even if you do not feel affected. Your employer's drug and alcohol policy applies. If you are unsure, ask HR before the test.
                          
    
                          
    
                          
      
                          🎒 What to bring
                          
      
                            
        
                          • Photo ID
                            • 
        
                          • Glasses, contact lenses or hearing aid
                            • 
        
                          • A list of your medicines, supplements and past injuries or operations
                            • 
        
                          • The booking details or form from the employer or agency
                            • 
        
                          • Water and a snack for afterwards
                            • 
      
                          
      
                          Answer every question truthfully. The medical is there to keep you safe on site. General information, not medical advice. Your clinic and employer decide what is needed.
                          
    
                          
  
                          
  
                          
    
                          🥥 Coconut oil morning routine
                          Oleation means rubbing a little oil over your skin before you shower. It leaves skin soft and helps it hold moisture in hot, dry and dusty work.
                          
    
                          
      
                          ⚠️ Not a sunscreen
                          
      
                          Coconut oil gives only very weak sun protection (about SPF 1 to 8 in tests). It does not replace proper sun protection. Australia's UV is very high, and oil on skin in strong sun can make burning worse. Use the oil before your shower, then put a zinc-based sunscreen on dry skin.
                          
    
                          
    
                          
      
                          ☀️ The best way, step by step 10 min
                          
      
                            
        
                          1. Patch test once. Rub a little on your inner arm and wait 24 hours. Skip it if you get a rash or itch.
                            2. 
        
                          2. Warm it. Scoop about 1 tablespoon of virgin (cold-pressed) coconut oil into your hands. It melts on contact with warm skin.
                            3. 
        
                          3. Rub it all over. Use long strokes on arms and legs and small circles on joints. Do 3 to 5 minutes, head to toe. A thin layer is enough.
                            4. 
        
                          4. Wait 5 minutes so it soaks in, then have a warm (not hot) shower. Use soap only where you need it, such as underarms, groin and feet.
                            5. 
        
                          5. Dry off, then zinc sunscreen. Pat dry, wait a few minutes, then put on a zinc-based (mineral) sunscreen, SPF 50+ and water-resistant, about 20 minutes before going outside. Reapply every 2 hours and after sweating. Add a hat, long sleeves and sunglasses.
                            6. 
      
                          
    
                          
    
                          
      
                          🧴 Zinc-based sunscreen options
                          
      
                          Zinc oxide is a natural mineral. It sits on top of your skin and blocks UV. Look for zinc oxide as the only active ingredient, SPF 50+, and read the full ingredient list on the label.
                          
      
                          WotNot Natural Sunscreen SPF 50+ (125 g)
                          Zinc oxide 22.7%. The rest is mostly plant oils and waxes (coconut oil, castor oil, candelilla wax, linseed oil, vitamin E). About A$28 at one Australian shop (RRP A$32.95). The page I checked did not say if it is non-nano or water resistant, so check the label. See listing
                          
      
                          WotNot Natural Zinc Sunscreen Stick SPF 50 (45 g)
                          Handy for nose, ears and cheeks on site. Check the label for the full ingredients. See listing
                          
      
                          Invisible Zinc Face + Body SPF 50
                          Zinc oxide is the only active, and it rubs in clear. It has a longer ingredient list that includes silicones and preservatives, so it is not all-natural. See ingredients
                          
      
                          Zinc can leave a white tint, so rub it in well. Ingredients and prices change, so always check the label. The TGA says zinc oxide nanoparticles in sunscreen are not likely to cause harm and found no cancer link (TGA). General information, not medical advice.
                          
    
                          
    
                          
      
                          🦺 Site and camp tips
                          
      
                            
        
                          • Skip your palms and soles before work, so gloves, tools and boots do not slip.
                            • 
        
                          • The floor gets slippery. Use a mat and dry your feet before you step out.
                            • 
        
                          • Face: coconut oil can clog pores. Leave your face out if you get pimples.
                            • 
        
                          • Wipe off extra oil with a towel before you shower. Do not wash lots of oil down camp drains.
                            • 
        
                          • Wash your towel often, because oil can make it smell.
                            • 
        
                          • Cold room? The oil goes solid below about 24°C. Warm it in your hands first.
                            • 
      
                          
      
                          Free sun safety and the daily UV index: SunSmart. See your GP about any new mole, spot or skin condition. General information, not medical advice.
                          
    
                          
  
                          
  
                          
    
                          🍺 Morning breath test (alcohol)
                          Many FIFO sites test your breath for alcohol at the start of a shift, at random, or before the bus or flight. Know the rules, and plan ahead if you drink.
                          
    
                          
      
                          What happens 1 min
                          
      
                            
        
                          • You blow steadily into a small mouthpiece for a few seconds.
                            • 
        
                          • Many sites have a zero limit (0.00) or a very low one. Check your site rules at induction, or ask your supervisor.
                            • 
        
                          • A positive result is often followed by a second test after a wait (commonly about 15 minutes). Then you may be stood down, sent home, or asked to talk to HR. It depends on the site policy.
                            • 
        
                          • Driving over 0.05 is illegal for most drivers, and some sites and buses are stricter.
                            • 
      
                          
    
                          
    
                          
      
                          🕐 If you are drinking, plan ahead
                          
      
                          Your body clears about 1 standard drink per hour. That is slow and it varies from person to person. Nothing speeds it up. Coffee, water, a cold shower, food, electrolytes and mouthwash do not remove alcohol. Mouthwash can even cause a reading itself.
                          
      
                            
        
                          • You can feel fine and still be over the limit. A big night often means alcohol is still in your system at 6am.
                            • 
        
                          • Count your drinks. One standard drink is about a 285 ml glass of full-strength beer, 100 ml of wine or 30 ml of spirits. Large pours and cocktails are often 2 or more.
                            • 
        
                          • Work backwards from your start time. Allow at least 1 hour for each standard drink after your last one, then add extra time to be safe. Do not cut it fine.
                            • 
        
                          • On a zero-limit site, the safest plan is no drinks in the hours before a test, or before a shift.
                            • 
        
                          • Phone apps and cheap personal breathalysers are not accurate. Do not rely on them.
                            • 
        
                          • Alcohol makes you pass more urine, so drink water alongside it. That helps how you feel, but it does not lower your reading. See Hydration.
                            • 
      
                          
    
                          
    
                          
      
                          🚨 If you test positive
                          
      
                            
        
                          • Stay calm and polite. Arguing makes it worse.
                            • 
        
                          • Follow the instructions. Do not drive and do not go onto site.
                            • 
        
                          • Be honest about what you drank and when.
                            • 
        
                          • Ask for the written drug and alcohol policy and what happens next.
                            • 
        
                          • Ask about the EAP: free, private counselling.
                            • 
        
                          • If you think the process was wrong, get advice. Try your union or the Fair Work Ombudsman 13 13 94.
                            • 
      
                          
    
                          
    
                          
      
                          💛 Worried about your drinking?
                          
      
                          Talk to someone, free and private:
                          
      
                          
        National Alcohol and Other Drug Hotline · 1800 250 015Free, confidential advice and referrals
        MATES · 1300 642 11124/7, for construction, mining and energy workers
      
                          
      
                          General information, not legal or medical advice. Your site policy decides what applies to you.
                          
    
                          
  
                          
  
                          
    
                          💧 Hydration and electrolytes
                          Heat, sweat, long shifts and dry camps use up your water and salts fast. Being low makes you tired, foggy and crampy, which is a safety risk on site.
                          
    
                          
      
                          ✅ Quick check
                          
      
                            
        
                          • Urine colour: pale straw means good. Dark yellow means drink more.
                            • 
        
                          • Start your shift hydrated. Drink a big glass when you wake up and with every meal.
                            • 
        
                          • Little and often: about a cup (250 ml) every 15 to 20 minutes when you work in the heat. Do not gulp litres at once.
                            • 
        
                          • Do not only drink huge amounts of plain water while sweating a lot. You also lose salt. Add salty food or an electrolyte drink.
                            • 
      
                          
      
                          Stop work, get to shade, cool down and tell your supervisor if you get a bad headache, dizziness, nausea, cramps or stop sweating. Call 000 if someone is confused, collapses or has hot, dry skin.
                          
    
                          
    
                          
      
                          🧂 Electrolyte drink options
                          
      
                          Amounts are from the product listings I checked on 11 Nov 2026. Always read the label. Prices change.
                          
      
                          LMNT Recharge (sold by Australian online shops)
                          Per 6 g stick: 1000 mg sodium, 200 mg potassium, 60 mg magnesium, no sugar. About A$64.95 for 30 sticks (about A$2.17 a stick). Very salty, good for heavy sweating. See listing
                          
      
                          Hydralyte Plus Sports (Woolworths, chemists)
                          Per 600 mL serve: 690 mg sodium, 353 mg potassium, 29 mg magnesium, 12 g glucose. Glucose helps your body absorb the salts. See listing
                          
      
                          Gastrolyte (chemists)
                          Per sachet: sodium chloride 470 mg, sodium acid citrate 530 mg, potassium chloride 300 mg, glucose 3.56 g. No magnesium listed. Made for rehydration after sickness or a big sweat. See listing
                          
      
                          Not sure about another brand? Check the label for sodium, potassium and magnesium per serve, and the sugar.
                          
    
                          
    
                          
      
                          🥜 Magnesium and potassium
                          
      
                            
        
                          • Drink mixes only give a small amount of magnesium (about 30 to 60 mg a serve). Adults need roughly 300 to 400 mg a day in total.
                            • 
        
                          • Food first: nuts, seeds, leafy greens, beans and wholegrains for magnesium. Bananas, potatoes, beans, dairy and coconut water for potassium.
                            • 
        
                          • Want a magnesium supplement? Ask a pharmacist. Too much can cause loose bowels.
                            • 
        
                          • Ask your GP or pharmacist first if you have kidney problems or take blood pressure tablets. High potassium and magnesium can be unsafe for some people.
                            • 
        
                          • Salty electrolyte drinks are not for everyone, for example if you have high blood pressure. Check with your doctor.
                            • 
      
                          
      
                          General information, not medical advice.
